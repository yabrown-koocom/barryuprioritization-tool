Why these placements

The math behind each quadrant, the items that contest the framework, and what the cabinet will actually debate.

BUILD TOWARD

Y ≥ 3 AND Time-Criticality < 3. Items with real strategic impact that aren't yet time-critical.

Strategic bets, multi-year academic launches, and capacity to grow into live here. Often under-populated when the cabinet is in firefighting mode.

Cabinet question: Are we under-investing in strategic optionality?

FUND NOW

Y ≥ 3 AND Time-Criticality ≥ 3. Revenue drivers and urgent unblockers — items that produce or directly enable near-term revenue and demand resources this cycle.

The crowding here is the central conversation: when many items compete for "fund now" treatment, the real prioritization happens INSIDE this quadrant — by Feasibility, dependency, and investment band.

Cabinet question: Which 5–7 actually get funded this cycle?

FUTURE PHASE

Y < 3 AND Time-Criticality < 3. Low strategic impact AND low urgency this cycle. Capital projects (Master Plan, Library, Beautification) sit here because they're real work but explicitly post-land-sale.

Sponsors of items in this quadrant will not want to see "Future Phase" — the framing language is the negotiation.

Cabinet question: This is the most politically sensitive quadrant. How do we honor stewardship without funding it this cycle?

SUSTAIN

Y < 3 AND Time-Criticality ≥ 3. Operational must-dos that are urgent but don't strongly drive revenue or outcomes on their own.

SIS Replacement is the bellwether here. Direct revenue scoring puts it in Sustain; including unblocker effects (online program scale depends on SIS) moves it to Fund Now. The framework's convention is to route enabling effects through Time-Criticality + Cost-of-Delay, keeping the Y-axis clean.

Convention

Revenue counts direct revenue only. Unblocker effects (e.g., SIS enabling online scale) flow through Time-Criticality via Cost-of-Delay, to keep the Y-axis from inflating.

Glossary

Terms used in the framework, the rubric, and the cabinet conversation. Worth circulating with the rubric so the room is using the same definitions.

Unblockernoun
An investment that doesn't produce revenue or measurable outcomes directly, but is required for another investment that does. Without it, a revenue driver can't scale, can't launch, or can't perform at the level its scores assume. SIS Replacement is the canonical example — Barry Online cannot reach its enrollment target without modern student information system functionality.
Cost-of-Delaynoun
The dollarized cost of waiting an additional month (or quarter) to complete an unblocker, expressed as the downstream revenue or outcome value that slips during the delay. Used to score unblockers on Time-Criticality without inflating their Revenue Impact. Test: "Each month SIS is delayed costs $X of online program scale potential."
Mission Preconditionnoun
The lightweight gate that confirms an item is aligned with Barry's Catholic Dominican mission before it enters the matrix. Items that fail the precondition are excluded entirely. Mission is not scored on the rubric — every cabinet member would rate every item a 5, collapsing the criterion. Instead, alignment is presumed for any item that passes the gate.
Run / Grow / Transformframework
The board-facing communication overlay borrowed from Gartner. The same items the cabinet scores in quadrants get regrouped for the board: Run (Sustain quadrant — keep-the-lights-on), Grow (Fund Now — revenue drivers and urgent unblockers), Transform (Build Toward — strategic bets, multi-year launches). Lets the board see the spending mix at a glance.
Strategic Impact (Y)axis
The vertical axis of the matrix. Computed as the weighted average of Revenue Impact and Student Outcome Impact, with weights set by the Triad before cabinet scoring. Default is 50/50.
Time-Criticality (X)axis
The horizontal axis. Scored 1–5 standalone (not a composite). Captures both raw urgency and Cost-of-Delay for unblockers. Score 5 means "blocking another priority now"; score 1 means "no urgency this cycle."
Feasibilitygate
The fourth criterion. Does not determine quadrant placement directly — it sorts items within a quadrant. Two Fund Now items with the same Y and Time score get prioritized by Feasibility (clean execution beats high execution risk). Can also be visualized as dot size on the chart.
Forced Distributionmechanic
An optional discipline borrowed from Eastern Washington University's resource allocation process. Requires a set percentage of total budget dollars to fall in each quadrant — preventing the Fund Now bucket from exceeding the actual budget by 2–3x. Not adopted by default; the Triad decides.
Stress Testtool
The Strict (3.5) / Lenient (2.5) preview buttons in this tool. Diagnostic only — shows which items are robustly strategic vs. marginally strategic by raising or lowering the quadrant boundary. Standard (3.0) is the framework default; the boundary doesn't move in cabinet-facing materials.
Overrideverb
An explicit decision by the Triad or cabinet to fund (or deprioritize) an item differently than its rubric placement suggests. Overrides are legitimate; the rubric is a tool, not a verdict. Boards accept overrides when documented with a reason — they distrust silent ones.
